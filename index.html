$/SF in historic luxury homes must be read carefully:
✦ Higher $/SF = full renovation — new mechanicals, kitchen, baths
✦ Lower $/SF = deferred maintenance or original condition
✦ The spread reflects real value or real capital requirement — usually both

⚠️ The Key Question

What explains the discount? If condition and needed updates are the answer, you need a renovation budget estimate. If the home is undiscovered and correctly priced as-is, that's a very different and more exciting story.

Questions to Ask · Strategic Takeaway

Smart Questions to Ask Before You Offer

1
What is the seller's motivation — and is there a timeline driving the decision?
2
Have there been any prior offers? If so, why did they fall through?
3
What is the age and condition of the HVAC, roof, plumbing, and electrical systems?
4
Are there any known defects, pending repairs, or deferred maintenance items disclosed?
5
Has the home had any major renovation since 1925, and are permits available?
6
What is the realistic cost to bring mechanicals and finishes to current market standards?
7
Is the property in a historic overlay or conservation district affecting renovation scope?
8
What did fully renovated comparable homes on this block actually cost to renovate?
